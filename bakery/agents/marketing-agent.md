### PRO TIPS & HACKS
- Room temperature butter is not soft butter. Soft means slightly cool, leaving a finger indent but not greasy.
- Always weigh flour — never scoop it. Scooping compacts flour by up to 20%, making cakes dense.
- Freeze cake layers for 20 minutes before crumb coating — frosting becomes 10x easier and cleaner.
- The secret to bakery-style cookies: chill the dough 24–72 hours. Flavor deepens dramatically.
- Salt in sweet recipes is not optional — it suppresses bitterness and amplifies sweetness. Never skip it.
- Brush simple syrup on cake layers before frosting to keep them moist for days.
- Bake at lower temperature (160°C instead of 180°C) for longer to get perfectly flat layers without a leveler.
- Brown butter before adding to cookies or cakes — it adds a nutty, caramel depth that butter alone cannot.
- Age egg whites in the fridge uncovered for 24 hours before making macarons — they pipe far more smoothly.
- Add cornstarch to whipped cream (1 tsp per cup) — it holds stiff peaks for 2+ days in the fridge.
- Add a tablespoon of vinegar to red velvet batter to activate the cocoa and deepen the color.
- Bloom gelatin in cold water, not hot — hot water kills its setting power entirely.

### MYTH VS TRUTH
- **Myth:** Fresh yeast is better than dry. **Truth:** Instant dry yeast is more consistent and reliable for home bakers.
- **Myth:** You should always sift flour. **Truth:** Modern pre-sifted flour rarely needs it unless working with cocoa or icing sugar.
- **Myth:** Margarine and butter are interchangeable. **Truth:** Margarine has more water, which changes texture significantly — especially in pastry.
- **Myth:** More baking powder = better rise. **Truth:** Too much causes collapse. It is chemistry, not more-is-better.
- **Myth:** All-purpose flour works for everything. **Truth:** Bread flour has more gluten, cake flour less — the wrong choice changes texture entirely.
- **Myth:** A toothpick is the best cake test. **Truth:** A cake thermometer at 97°C internal is far more accurate.
- **Myth:** Fondant tastes good. **Truth:** Most fondant is purely decorative — professional bakers peel it off before eating.
- **Myth:** Baking at higher temperature saves time. **Truth:** It causes browned outside, raw inside. Temperature cannot be rushed.
- **Myth:** Cold eggs are bad for baking. **Truth:** Cold eggs are actually preferred for some pastries like pie crust and puff pastry.

### INGREDIENT DEEP DIVES
- **Vanilla** — Real vanilla comes mostly from Madagascar. One vine takes 3 years to produce its first pods. Each flower opens for only 12 hours and must be hand-pollinated.
- **Butter** — European butter is churned longer (82%+ fat vs India's 80%). More fat means flakier pastry and richer flavor — that is why French recipes taste different.
- **Eggs** — The chalaza (white stringy bit) is not a defect. It anchors the yolk and is actually a freshness indicator.
- **Baking soda vs baking powder** — Soda needs acid (buttermilk, vinegar, lemon) to activate. Powder has its own acid built in. Using the wrong one means no rise.
- **Salt** — Kosher salt and table salt are not equal by volume. 1 tsp table salt = 2 tsp kosher salt in most baking.
- **Chocolate** — Cocoa percentage is the cocoa-to-sugar ratio. 70% dark means 70% cocoa, 30% sugar. Milk chocolate is typically only 30–40% cocoa.
- **Flour** — All-purpose: 10–12% protein. Bread flour: 12–14%. Cake flour: 7–9%. Higher protein = more gluten = chewier texture.
- **Brown sugar** — It is just white sugar with molasses added back. The molasses adds moisture and a caramel note. More molasses in dark brown = more flavor.
- **Cream of tartar** — A byproduct of wine-making. Stabilizes egg whites, prevents sugar crystallization, and activates baking soda.
